## Part 3 — WhatsApp Business Platform: cost, architecture, constraints

### Pricing model

Per-message since **1 July 2025**; conversation-based pricing formally deprecated.
Four categories: Marketing, Utility, Authentication, Service.

Lebanon bills under **"Rest of Middle East"** (with Bahrain, Iraq, Jordan, Kuwait,
Oman, Qatar, Yemen). Rates effective 1 July 2026: Marketing **$0.03922**, Utility
**$0.01047**, Authentication **$0.01047**, Service **free**.

Regional comparison: UAE $0.0574 / $0.0181 · Saudi $0.0576 / $0.0123 · Egypt $0.0740 / $0.0041.

**Rate sources disagree.** SetSmart (25 Jul 2026) lists UAE marketing at $0.0816 against
SleekFlow's $0.0574. HighLevel's July 2026 changelog gives Rest of Middle East as
$0.0358 / $0.0096. The authentication figure rests on a single source. Meta warns rates
may update quarterly. **Fetch rates; do not hard-code them.**

### What is free

1. All non-template messages inside an open 24-hour service window — unlimited.
2. Utility templates inside an open window (since 1 July 2025).
3. Everything, any category, inside a **72-hour Free Entry Point window** — opened by a
   Click-to-WhatsApp ad or Facebook Page CTA when replied to within 24h.
4. Cloud API access itself — Meta hosts it, no infrastructure fee.
5. All inbound messages.

**Obsolete figure to watch for:** "1,000 free service conversations per month" was
replaced on **1 November 2024** when service conversations became unconditionally free
with no cap. Several blogs still repeat it.

### The 24-hour service window, precisely

- A customer message opens the window; the timer resets on each new inbound message.
- **Open:** any free-form message, unlimited, free. Utility and authentication templates
  free. **Marketing templates are charged even inside the window.**
- **Closed:** free-form messages are **rejected by the API**. Templates only, charged.
- **Never replied:** no window at all. Template-only, charged, and opt-in required.

### Onboarding

Requires a Meta Business Portfolio; a phone number **not active on any WhatsApp app**;
display name approval; template approval; Meta Business Verification for scale.

- **Unverified businesses can still send** — capped at **250 unique customers per rolling
  24 hours**. For a wholesaler doing 200 messages a month this is never a constraint,
  so verification can be deferred past first value.
- **Messaging limits** scale 250 → 2,000 → 10k → 100k → unlimited on quality and usage.
- **Display name** blocked until approved; sending fails with error **#131037**.
- **Business Verification** accepts documents in ~21 languages including **Arabic and
  French**. Timeline: "minutes to several days." No reliable published median found.
- **Template approval:** minutes to hours, no SLA. Top rejection cause is category
  mismatch — marketing content submitted as Utility.

**Coexistence** (global, May 2026) lets a business keep its number on the WhatsApp
Business App while the API runs alongside, importing up to 6 months of 1:1 history.
Requires Business App ≥ v2.24.17, a live physical device, opened once every 14 days.
Permanently disables Broadcast Lists, Disappearing Messages, View Once and Live Location
for 1:1 chats. Group chats do not sync.

### Partner models

- **Solution Partner** — has a Meta credit line, can bill clients for usage itself.
- **Tech Provider** — no credit line; **each client attaches their own payment method and
  Meta bills them directly.** Requires App Review for Advanced access.

A WABA belongs to exactly one business portfolio and may be shared with up to two
partners. Under Embedded Signup the WABAs live in the clients' portfolios, so the
20-WABA-per-portfolio ceiling does not cap tenant count.

### Unofficial libraries — the risk

Baileys, whatsapp-web.js, WAHA, Evolution API reverse-engineer WhatsApp Web and puppet a
linked device. The WhatsApp Business Terms forbid reverse engineering, scraping, and
*"develop or use any applications that interact with our Business Services without our
prior written consent."* Every library in this class is inside that prohibition.

Detection is pattern-based; practitioner reporting describes bans as a matter of when,
typically within weeks. **No credible published ban-rate statistics exist** — this is
consistent practitioner consensus, not measured data.

The consequence is asymmetric and lands on the client: a ban takes out **the wholesaler's
primary business number**, the one on their invoices and shopfront, with years of history.
A banned number cannot later be migrated to the official API until the ban is appealed.

### Lebanon specifically

- **Not on Meta's restricted-country list.** That list is Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria,
  and Crimea/Donetsk/Luhansk. Note: messaging **to Syrian numbers is blocked**, which is
  a real scenario for Beirut wholesalers.
- **Verification documents are workable** — Arabic and French accepted. Whether reviewers
  accept the Lebanese Commercial Register extract (*Sijill Tijari*) is unverified.
- **Payment is the real blocker.** Stripe does not support Lebanon-registered businesses;
  currency is fragmented across lollar / fresh dollar / LBP. **Whether Meta billing accepts
  Lebanese-issued cards could not be verified.** Highest-value open question.
- **Lebanese A2P SMS is corroborated as broken.** [SMEX, 6 Feb 2025](https://smex.org/lebanons-telecom-feud-disrupts-sms-verification-system/)
  documents failure on both carriers — Alfa a full year without A2P payments to its
  contractor, touch stalled after six cancelled bids. WhatsApp, WhatsApp Business, X and
  OTP delivery all affected. **No 2026 update found** — the status today is unconfirmed.
  WhatsApp number verification accepts **voice call** as well as SMS; steer onboarding to
  voice. Coexistence largely routes around the problem entirely.

## Part 4 — Scope resolution evidence

### Duplicate detection

**Premise corrections.** Dubsado has **no** duplicate detection and **no** merge —
*"There is no way to merge two clients"*, and bulk import *"will create a new entry for
each of the clients on that list, duplicating any clients who already exist."*
Attio has **no** native duplicate detection and **no** bulk merge. HubSpot's real tool is
Professional+ and capped at 2,000/5,000/10,000 pairs reviewed one at a time.

**How it is actually implemented.** Exact match on a designated identifier is the norm;
fuzzy matching is gated behind paid tiers. HubSpot auto-dedups on **exact email string**
only. Zoho enforces unique fields and **hard-blocks the save**. Salesforce separates
*alert* (up to 15 matches, user may save anyway) from *block*. Dynamics warns, never
blocks. **Less Annoying CRM** — the closest comparable — shows a red warning at entry on
name, email or phone, linking to the existing record, and nothing more.

**Documented failure modes.** Zoho's hard block produces this, from Zoho's own community:
*"Multiple employees at the same company sometimes share email addresses… Should I put
the contact in without an email address in this case?"* Blocking teaches users to strip
identifiers. Merge is irreversible everywhere: HubSpot *"It's not possible to unmerge
records"*, with merged records unenrolled from all workflows and every property timestamp
reset; Bigin *"deleted permanently and the action cannot be reverted"*; Attio *"permanently
deleted"*; OnePageCRM *"cannot be undone"* with advice to export first.

**Phone normalisation is load-bearing.** Exact-string matching fails on formatting — the
same number as `(555) 123-4567`, `+1 555-123-4567` and `555.123.4567` becomes three
contacts. E.164 normalisation with trunk-prefix removal is the fix. The Lebanon-specific
mapping (`03`/`70`/`+961`/`00961`) is an inference from the general evidence, not a
sourced local finding — but a cheap and low-risk one.

**Switching evidence: none found.** A full Capterra page of Zoho CRM reviews mentions
duplicates, merging and cleanup **zero times**. Reddit was not reachable through the
research tooling — a genuine gap. Duplicate detection reads as an annoyance suppressor,
not a purchase driver.

### Bulk delete and undo

**Five vendors independently built import-scoped rollback**, which is the strongest
available evidence that imports are what goes wrong:

| Vendor | Rollback | Window |
|---|---|---|
| Salesforce | Document titled *Using Mass Delete to Undo Imports* | — |
| Zoho CRM | Undo import from Import History; **added rows only, not updated** | 30 days |
| Pipedrive | Revert from import history, **global admins only** | 48 hours |
| HubSpot | Restore CRM changes by source = Import | 14 days |
| Dubsado | "Remove Project and Client Imports" — everything ever imported | unlimited, blunt |

**Safeguards vendors converged on:** HubSpot requires typing the record count before
deleting, plus an email receipt. LACRM forces an explicit "Permanently delete" choice
with the count. Soft-delete windows: HubSpot 90 days, Zoho 60, Pipedrive 30, GoHighLevel
60 — GHL restorable **by bulk-action batch**, so the operation is undone rather than
individual records hunted. Attio is the counter-example: bulk delete with *"once a record
is deleted, it cannot be recovered by you or your team."*

**The point that decides it for a free product:** LACRM has no recycle bin at all —
recovery means emailing support, who restore from backups. That model requires a support
team. This product has none, so the undo must be in the software.

**No named disaster case was found.** The five-vendor convergence on rollback is the
substitute evidence. **No evidence at all was found on mobile bulk-action UX**, which
matters here — the mobile safeguards proposed are reasoning, not citation.

### Recurring tasks

**17hats' own published examples** of recurring to-dos: *"Refresh your 17hats Bank
Connection", "Fully Clean out inbox", "17hats Account Clean Up", "Reconcile Bookkeeping",
"Follow up with all cold leads"* — four of five are admin. They attach to **to-do lists**,
not to clients.

**Zoho** implements it properly as a CRM feature (daily/weekly/monthly/yearly/custom,
end never / after N / on date, linked to Contacts, Leads, Accounts). **HubSpot** now ships
repeating tasks on all plans — after Ideas requests open since **November 2017** and
**January 2020**. The workarounds users described in the meantime: manually adjusting 462
imported tasks, copy-pasting task titles one by one, keeping the whole thing in Excel.

**The two closest comparables ship without it.** LACRM repeats *events* only — its task
documentation covers creation, assignment, templates and bulk edit, with no recurrence.
OnePageCRM has Next / Scheduled / Queued / ASAP / Waiting-For actions and **no recurrence
at all**; its "exactly one next action per contact" model is a substitute, not a gap.

**For wholesale, the need is a computed signal.** Distribution-CRM literature names it as
*"customers at-risk for churning, those who haven't ordered in a long time, large
customers whose spend has declined."* Pipedrive ships **Rotting** — a per-stage day
threshold from last update, resetting on any activity, note, file or email, tile turning
red. Capsule ships *Contacts you haven't contacted recently* (`Last Contacted On is
before`) and *Contacts that haven't been Updated*. LACRM ships a last-updated filter.

The one genuinely wholesale-shaped case for recurrence is the **call cycle** — a fixed
visit cadence per outlet — which field-sales tools model as an **account-level attribute**,
not a repeating to-do. That is one column, not a scheduler.

**Abandonment evidence: none found.** HubSpot ran roughly seven years with the request
open, at scale, without visible attributable churn. Capterra's Zoho review page mentions
recurring tasks zero times.

## Part 5 — Where the evidence ran out

Stated plainly, because a reviewer should know which parts are solid and which are not.

1. **Whether Meta billing accepts Lebanese-issued cards.** The highest-value unknown.
   Blocks nothing until FEAT-0014. Test with one real client.
2. **Whether Lebanese A2P SMS is still broken.** Best evidence is February 2025.
3. **WhatsApp authentication rate for Rest of Middle East** — single source.
4. **Meta acceptance of Lebanese Commercial Register documents** — no direct evidence.
5. **Median Business Verification turnaround** — "minutes to several days" is all
   anyone publishes.
6. **Any explicit Meta rule banning one shared number across many businesses.** The
   prohibition is inferred from Terms clauses on misrepresentation and resale, not from
   a named policy line.
7. **Ban rates for unofficial WhatsApp libraries** — practitioner consensus, not data.
8. **Folk's native WhatsApp claim** — first-party marketing only.
9. **Zoho's and Salesforce Starter's exact WhatsApp edition thresholds.**
10. **CSV export terms** for Pipedrive, Attio, Bitrix24, monday, Freshsales, Dubsado,
    HoneyBook, Bonsai, 17hats, Salesforce Starter — not verified per product.
11. **Lebanon-specific CRM competition.** Search coverage is US-weighted. "No local
    competitor" is not established and should not be asserted.
12. **Switching evidence for duplicate detection and recurring tasks** — none found on
    G2, Capterra or vendor forums. Reddit was not reachable through the tooling.
13. **Mobile bulk-action UX** in CRMs — no literature found.
14. **No named bulk-delete disaster case** — only the safeguards vendors built afterwards.
